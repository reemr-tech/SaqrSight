## 6. What this shows

- Adding Gulf-dialect and everyday wording (lexicon 1.1) raised recall on unseen posts without adding false alarms: negation, quotations, debunks and official warnings were still not flagged.
- Fear / alarm wording remains the weakest category: it is expressed in many different ways ("people are trapped", "something bad is happening") that a word list cannot cover fully.
- With about 90 training posts, the machine-learning model found fewer indicators overall than the transparent rules. Machine learning needs more labelled data; the comparison should be repeated on the real, reviewed posts.
- The machine-learning model did better than the rules on: Fear / alarm in English (F1 0.55 vs 0.40); Fear / alarm in Arabic (F1 0.55 vs 0.40). Because it learns from letter patterns rather than fixed phrases, it generalises better to categories with very varied wording – a combined approach (rules + model) is worth testing in Senior Project 2.
- The rules show the exact words behind every finding, which is what the report needs to explain itself to the public.
